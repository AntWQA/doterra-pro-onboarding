## Card export shadows, and why chroma-keying was abandoned

Every card here has its drop shadow applied as a Figma effect on the exported node, and this file's PNG export has **no alpha at all**: Figma renders each node onto an opaque **white** matte (`alpha=255` uniformly) with extra padding for the shadow's bleed.

An earlier pass tried to remove that matte by chroma-keying white and zeroing alpha where it matched, tuning the threshold per asset. **That approach cannot work here and has been removed entirely.** The matte is white and the cards' own fill is also white, so no threshold can separate them — a threshold loose enough to clear the matte punches straight through the card interior, and one tight enough to spare the interior leaves the matte. Measuring the shipped assets found white interiors destroyed in 14 of 21 files, with the eaten pixels averaging RGB (254,254,254):

| asset | white px destroyed |
|---|---|
| `tracking-fast-start` | 357,354 |
| `pro-advisor-1` | 345,969 |
| `toast-notification` | 279,549 |
| `my-team-row-thompson` | 192,592 |
| `tasks-dropdown` | 182,474 |

That is why cards rendered as translucent ghosts with the gradient showing through them, and why `insight-sub-card` was invisible on Frame 6 (91.8% of it had been erased).

**Current method — `scripts/reexport_align.py`.** Crop purely by geometry; never touch a single pixel's alpha. The script locates the existing asset inside the fresh raw export by cross-correlating their *dark* pixels (text and strokes, which the keying left intact), then crops to those bounds. It is self-verifying: a correct crop scores ~100% dark-pixel overlap and the script refuses to write below 99%. All 21 assets were re-exported this way and every one scored 100.0%. Shadows are real CSS `box-shadow`, and rounded corners are handled with CSS `border-radius` on the `<img>` — the matte that remains in a corner is white, which is invisible against the white cards and page, and is clipped away on the coloured ones.

**A crop must never be BIGGER than its node either.** Three assets came through 5-16px taller than the node they were exported from — `pro-advisor-1` (+15 rows), `pro-advisor-2-bubble` (+5) and `toast-notification` (+16) — because the align step preserved the previous asset's bounds, and those bounds had swallowed part of Figma's baked shadow padding. Under the old chroma-key that strip was alpha-zeroed and therefore invisible; with exports now staying opaque it renders as a pale band below the card. Worse, the frames put a CSS `border-radius` and `box-shadow` on the `<img>`, so the browser rounds and shadows the *image box* rather than the card — producing a second rounded card edge offset below and right of the real one. That is what "the Meet Pro Advisor card looks stacked" was. All three are re-cropped to exact node bounds (the card always starts at row 0 of the raw; the shadow hangs off the bottom, so trimming the height moves nothing), and `reexport_align.py` now warns whenever a crop exceeds its node.

Two assets needed their bounds *widened* rather than preserved, because the key had eaten the card's outer margin and shrunk the bbox itself: `insight-sub-card` (892x817 for a node that is 999x885 at 3x — it had lost its rounded corners and entire bottom action row) and the unused `my-team-card`. For those the raw node export is used wholesale; `insight-sub-card`'s repositioning offset was measured off the red "193 days overdue" pill, identical in size in both exports.

**`scripts/figma-nodes.json` now records the node ID and declared size for every asset**, so none of this has to be rediscovered.

## Not evaluated this pass

Because this build treats every screen as a frozen visual export rather than a rebuilt component tree, defects that would only surface in a DOM/accessibility audit (contrast, focus order, ARIA labelling of exported images) were not assessed — the exported PNGs are decorative `alt=""` images throughout, with real headings/body copy as the only text-content that's genuinely in the accessibility tree.
